United States - labour authority: Department of Labor (DOL), Employment and Training Administration (ETA),
Office of Foreign Labor Certification (OFLC)
Collected 15 November 2026. Script: download_us_labour.py (python3 download_us_labour.py).

Role
OFLC decides labor certifications: PERM (permanent), H-2A and H-2B temporary labor certifications, prevailing
wage determinations (National Prevailing Wage Center), and certifies H-1B/H-1B1/E-3 Labor Condition
Applications. State Workforce Agencies (SWAs) process H-2A/H-2B job orders, inspect H-2A housing and run
prevailing-wage surveys under OFLC grant guidance. USCIS then decides the petitions.

What was selected
OFLC's "Policies and Regulations" page (capture of 8 August 2026,
https://www.dol.gov/agencies/eta/foreign-labor/policy) says OFLC "occasionally issues policy directives to help
employees of the Department of Labor and SWAs, and also Employers ... understand the procedures for filing and
adjudicating applications". Taken: every directive listed there (TEGLs 11-07 Change 2, 16-06 Change 1, 17-06
Change 1, 33-10, 21-06 Change 1, 31-05, 27-06; the PERM program guidance bulletin; the Prevailing Wage
Determination Policy Guidance, revised November 2009) plus the base TEGLs and attachments they amend, TEGLs
15-06 and 32-10 (same series), the earlier (May 2005) prevailing-wage guidance, the August 2008 restatement of
the PERM bulletin, ET Handbook No. 385 (Wage Finding Process, August 1981, SWA survey manual), two 2005
DFLC directives to the processing-centre directors (Hurricane Katrina field memorandum; hurricanes TEGL), the
annual Foreign Labor Certification grant planning TEGLs to SWAs (15-17, 14-19, 21-20, 12-21 with Changes 1
and 2, 15-24 with Change 1), and the H-1B Program Bulletin of 15 January 2021 on secondary employers.
Not taken (applicant information or law): OFLC FAQs (hundreds of rounds), webinars, fact sheets, filing tips,
user guides, forms, Federal Register rules and notices, press-style disaster announcements after 2005,
statistics. The March 2010 "NPWHC" file is an FAQ, not the policy guidance, and was left out.

Saved: 43 PDFs, 46 MB, all kind guidance, dated 1981-2026. All checked with pdfinfo. Many pre-2012 TEGLs are
scans without a text layer.

Temporary-work agencies, payroll companies, employers of record; EU Blue Card
- H-1B Program Bulletin "Clarifying Filing Requirements for Labor Condition Applications by Secondary Employers
  at 20 C.F.R. 655.715 and 655.730(a)", OFLC, 15 January 2021: OFLC "now interprets the regulations to require
  secondary common-law employers of H-1B workers to file a" Labor Condition Application; it describes
  "staffing agencies (primary employers) that petition to hire H-1B workers, as well as staffing agencies'
  clients (secondary employers) where the H-1B workers are assigned to work. While the primary employer
  typically serves as the H-1B worker's employer for payroll and tax purposes, the secondary employer often
  exercises considerable control over the worker's day-to-day work." The archive shows the file still on
  dol.gov in January 2026; its current legal status was not checked.
- TEGL 21-06 (4 April 2007), Attachment A, part III "Application Filing Procedures", C (repeated in Change 1,
  25 June 2007): "Job contractors typically supply labor to one or more employers as part of signed work
  contracts or labor services agreements. The temporary or permanent nature of the work to be performed in such
  applications will be determined by examining the job contractor's need for such workers, rather than the
  needs of its employer customers."
- The EU Blue Card does not apply to the United States.

How much real guidance
A fair amount, but old: OFLC's published directives are mostly 2005-2011 TEGLs on H-2A/H-2B special
procedures, the 2009 prevailing-wage guidance and the SWA grant TEGLs. OFLC's internal adjudication
procedures for its National Processing Centers (PERM audit, H-2A/H-2B review) are not published; current
practice is otherwise communicated through public FAQs and Federal Register rules.
